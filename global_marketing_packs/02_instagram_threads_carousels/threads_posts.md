## Post 1: The Bedtime Snack Trap (Viral Contrarian)
Giving your child a bowl of cereal or warm milk with cookies right before bed to "help them fall asleep" is accidentally suppressing their natural growth hormone.

Here's the physiology:
When blood glucose rises, insulin spikes.
Insulin directly triggers Somatostatin—which shuts down the pituitary gland from releasing human Growth Hormone (GH).

65% of daily GH is secreted during the first 90 minutes of deep slow-wave sleep between 10 PM and 2 AM. 

If blood sugar is elevated, that surge is muted.
Establish a strict 2-hour carb fast before bed. Water or calming chamomile only.

## Post 2: The Math Word Problem Myth (Educational Neuro-Science)
If your 8-year-old crushes fast arithmetic calculations but completely freezes on paragraph word problems, stop testing them on reading comprehension.

It's not a reading problem.
It's a Prefrontal Working Memory buffer overflow.

The human working memory holds 4 ± 1 chunks of data.
A paragraph word problem forces the brain to decode text, filter fluff words, track time sequences, and remember the numbers simultaneously. The brain freezes from cognitive overload.

The fix:
Have them draw a simple 10-second margin rectangle on the page to hold the numbers before writing any equation.
Offload memory to paper. Watch the anxiety vanish.
