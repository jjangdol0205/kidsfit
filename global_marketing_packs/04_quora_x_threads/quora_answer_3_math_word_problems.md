## Answer Body:

**Short Answer:**  
It is almost never a reading comprehension deficiency or a mathematical calculation problem. 

Instead, smart children freeze on math word problems because of **Prefrontal Working Memory Buffer Overflow**. Their working memory (which holds only 4 ± 1 chunks of data) becomes completely exhausted by textual parsing before they can retrieve their mathematical algorithms. 

Teaching children to draw a **10-second margin rectangle (Tape Diagram)** instantly offloads memory to paper, reducing cognitive load by over 60%.

---

### The Prefrontal Cognitive Bottleneck
In cognitive neuroscience (Baddeley's Model of Working Memory), the prefrontal cortex acts as a temporary mental scratchpad with limited capacity:
- In pure calculation (`36 + 18`), the child utilizes all 4 slots purely for arithmetic bonds.
- In a word problem, the child must simultaneously:
  1. Decode multi-clause sentences.
  2. Suppress narrative distractions ("Johnny bought 3 green apples from the market...").
  3. Track time sequence and pronoun references.
  4. Hold intermediate mathematical values in mind.

The prefrontal buffer overloads, leading to sudden cognitive freezing, emotional frustration, or random guessing.

---

### The 10-Second Margin Bar Fix (CRA Framework)
Train the child to follow a 3-step visual offloading strategy:
1. **Blue Box the Quantities**: Box numerical data in blue pencil.
2. **Red Circle the Target**: Circle the exact question goal in red.
3. **Draw the Margin Bar Model**: In the page margin, draw a rectangular bar representing the Total, and divide it into Part A and Part B before writing any mathematical equations.

This translates verbal statements into a spatial graphic processed by the right parietal lobe, freeing the prefrontal cortex to solve the calculation effortlessly.
